# Authoring rules: vision

Read this before editing `specs/vision.md`. Do not copy these rules into the spec.

The vision is the north star. Every other spec and feature must trace back to it.

Keep the file under 500 words. Replace every `<!-- fill in -->`. Delete unused rows.

## Elevator pitch

One or two sentences. State the core player action and why it is compelling. A reader must understand it without the rest of the document. Shape: "A \<genre\> where you \<core action\> to \<goal\>, set in \<world\>."

## High-level description

One paragraph, at most 150 words. Cover the core loop, the setting, the tone, and what makes the game distinct. Describe the experience. Do not write a feature list. Stay consistent with the elevator pitch.

## Target audience and player fantasy

- **Audience**: who plays this. Name experience level, play habits, and session length. "Everyone" is not an audience.
- **Player fantasy**: what the player gets to feel or be. A feeling or a role, not a mechanic.

## Essential qualities

At most **5**. Each quality is testable: a reviewer can judge whether a feature supports or undermines it.

Keep three rows. Add rows 4 and 5 only when needed. Delete unused rows.

Each row has a name, a one-line description, and a "supports it if…" test. The test is observable: two reviewers would reach the same judgment on a given feature. Qualities are distinct, and they follow from the pitch and the description.

## Tradeoffs

When qualities conflict, the higher-priority one wins. List pairs in priority order: quality over quality, then why. Every likely conflict has a stated winner. The order must not cycle.

## Non-goals

What this game is not. Features that only serve a non-goal are rejected. State at least one non-goal. Non-goals must not contradict the pitch, the description, or the qualities.
